# Kollektiv

**Kollektiv is the place where people learn a field from the people in it, build visible proof of their potential, and find work that fits.**

**Tagline:** *Find your people. Build your path.*

Kollektiv is not a generic social network with job posts bolted on. It is a career ecosystem organized around fields of work. A person can arrive knowing very little about a field, understand how it works, meet the people shaping it, create a body of real evidence, and get connected to jobs or paid opportunities.

The name comes from “collective”: progress is social. People get further when practical knowledge, generous feedback, and opportunity circulate within a community.

## The problem

Starting or changing a career is fragmented:

- Information is scattered across search results, videos, online courses, and closed group chats.
- LinkedIn rewards polished self-promotion more than meaningful contribution.
- Job boards reduce people to keywords, titles, and credentials.
- Early-career people often cannot get experience without a job—and cannot get a job without experience.
- Employers struggle to see the capability behind a résumé.

Kollektiv closes this gap by making learning, participation, evidence, and opportunities one connected journey.

## The promise

**For members:** “I can understand my field, belong in the conversation, and be discovered for what I can do.”

**For employers:** “I can meet candidates who have already shown curiosity, judgment, and practical potential.”

## Who it serves first

The first Kollektiv should be for people entering, growing in, or changing into a single field. The best launch field is **Product Design and UX** because it has an active online learning culture, portfolio-based hiring, clear mentors, and a natural need for peer feedback.

The first four member types:

| Member | Their need | What Kollektiv gives them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explorer | “Is this field for me?” | Clear field guides, real stories, starter paths |
| Emerging professional | “How do I get credible?” | Feedback, projects, contribution history, entry-level roles |
| Practitioner | “How do I keep learning and giving back?” | Smart discussion, reputation, mentoring, collaboration |
| Hiring partner | “Who can actually do this work?” | Context-rich talent discovery and warm introductions |

Once the model works in design, Kollektiv can expand field by field: data, marketing, healthcare, climate, skilled trades, finance, education, and more.

## The Kollektiv experience

### 1. Field Home — know what matters

Each field has a living home, not a noisy feed.

- A plain-language overview of the field and its career paths
- A “what’s changing” digest curated by experienced practitioners
- Glossaries that turn jargon into accessible language
- Starter, growing, and advanced learning paths
- Skill maps that explain what employers actually expect
- Roles, compensation ranges, pathways, and real career stories

This makes Kollektiv useful before someone has a large network or polished profile.

### 2. Circles — talk with the right people

Circles are topic- or stage-based groups within a field: UX research, portfolios, junior designers, design systems, healthcare UX, freelance practice, and so on.

Members can:

- Ask questions and receive structured, thoughtful answers
- Share work-in-progress safely
- Join critique sessions and office hours
- Form short-term study or project groups
- Follow useful threads and save field knowledge
- Recognize generous, high-quality contributions

The central design rule is **signal over spectacle**. Kollektiv does not reward follower count or outrage. It rewards usefulness, clarity, generosity, and evidence.

### 3. Proof of Practice — make potential visible

Every member has a Kollektiv Profile: a living record of where they are headed and the work that supports it.

It includes:

- Skills they are learning and skills they can demonstrate
- Projects, case studies, work samples, and reflections
- Relevant community contributions, such as a strong answer or useful critique
- Recommendations and skill acknowledgements from peers or mentors
- Learning milestones and completed field challenges
- Career goals and work preferences, controlled by the member

This is not a résumé clone. It is an evidence layer: a person’s thinking and growth are visible, with the member deciding what is public.

### 4. Opportunities — turn momentum into work

Opportunities are integrated with the field community rather than hidden behind a separate job board.

- Jobs, internships, apprenticeships, fellowships, freelance projects, and paid challenges
- Clear salary/rate ranges and expectations from every employer
